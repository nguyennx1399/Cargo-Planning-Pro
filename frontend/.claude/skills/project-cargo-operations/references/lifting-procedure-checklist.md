# Lifting procedure, precautions & roles

Source: BBC Guideline v1.0 (2009) §1.4. Script: `stability_calcs.hoisting_angle_critical_load_pct`.

## The three angles to watch
| Angle | Limit |
|---|---|
| Heel φ of the ship | ≤ ±3° without stability pontoons; ≤ ±1° with pontoons |
| Luffing angle α of the boom | stay above the minimum for load P + R = SWL (often crane-controlled) |
| Hoisting angle δ (hoist wire off vertical) | ≈0 single crane; ≤ 3° in dual-crane work |

Sliding risk when picking up from / setting down on a barge or quay: a tilted hoist pulls the unit sideways.
With friction μ = 0.3 the unit starts to slide once the hook carries ≈95 % (δ 1°), 90 % (2°), 85 % (3°) of its
mass — generally `T/W = μ / (sin δ + μ·cos δ)`. **Rule: while the hook carries ≥ 80 % of the weight and the unit
still touches the barge/quay (≤ 20 % resting), keep δ at zero** using heeling tanks and/or luffing.

## Before the lift — ship preparation (consider all)
- Load ballast/bunkers as needed; press up tanks that should be full (overflow at least twice) and sound tanks
  that should be empty — don't trust remote gauges. Avoid free surfaces.
- Where useful (source example lifts over port): stow tween-deck pontoons in the lower hold on the side away
  from the lift; shift or consume fuel so weight sits away from the lift side, compensating with ballast on
  the lift side. Fill the heeling tanks on the **lift side** and empty those opposite, so S can be pumped
  away from the load as it swings out.
- Limit open weather-deck hatch covers / land pontoon covers; lower anchors to the bottom if necessary.
- Single-crane job: put the other crane in sea position. Empty the swimming pool.
- Close openings near the waterline, take the gangway in, keep moorings tended (neither slack nor tight).

## Before the lift — organisation
- Complete the SMS lifting checklist; hold a pre-operation meeting: sequence, communications, duties, visitor
  safety zones.
- Visually check all gear before assembly and check its certificates.
- Auxiliary power arranged, diesel day tanks full. Radios charged and tested; tag lines ready.
- Daylight only, unless agreed with BBC and extra supervision provided. Short debrief afterwards.

## Roles
- **Officer in charge**: directs crane drivers, pump operator and deck crew; experienced (trained under supervision).
- **Crane driver(s)** / **pump operator**: trained on their consoles, fit, know the command set.
- **Duty engineer**: controls power supply. **Deck crew**: fit for duty.
- **Overall surveillance** (preferably the master in delicate lifts): watches the three angles, wind, swell,
  passing traffic, moorings; keeps records/photos.
- **Port Captain** (if assigned): assists planning, execution and documentation.

## Loading sequence
1. Barge/vehicle alongside no further out than the planned outreach a.
2. Connect with δ = 0; take a small part of the load, stop.
3. Pump ballast to the side away from the load and/or luff up so δ returns to zero; keep hoisting in small
   steps, always correcting δ, until the unit hangs free (bulk of ballast now on the far side).
4. Slew inboard in short steps while pumping ballast back; unit ends over the ship, then lower to stowage.
5. Dual crane: never slew both consoles simultaneously — one after the other, checking δ continuously.

## Unloading sequence
1. Receiver alongside within planned outreach. Remove all lashings and vertical clamps; horizontal stoppers
   may stay with face plates removed.
2. δ = 0 before lifting; lift a few cm while still between stoppers, then adjust δ to protect adjacent cargo.
3. Once clear of the coaming, slew out in short steps while pumping ballast to the opposite side.
4. At the outboard position lower until it just touches (≈2–5 t eased off the hook), stop, then pump ballast back
   and/or luff down keeping δ ≈ 0.
5. Below 80 % of the weight on the hook, δ up to 3° is tolerable — but in any harbour swell avoid any δ.
